# Chess Education Missions

GambitNet is most useful when a player brings a question to the board. These missions turn the arena into a lightweight practice partner. They do not require engine evaluation or perfect notation.

## Beginner missions

- [ ] Point to the center before move ten. Which pieces influence it?
- [ ] Name every developed piece and identify one undeveloped piece after the opening.
- [ ] Before each capture, ask: what is defended, and what recaptures?
- [ ] Find the first moment when either king becomes vulnerable.

## Improving-player missions

- [ ] Predict the most urgent move before the motivation round resolves.
- [ ] Classify three agent choices as a check, capture, threat, improvement, or waiting move.
- [ ] After a queen trade, identify the strongest king route or pawn break.
- [ ] Compare two teams with one personality change and write a three-game hypothesis.

## Discussion prompts

Use these after a game with a friend, coach, or study group:

1. Which piece had the clearest reason to move?
2. Did the team's move match the stated motivation?
3. Which legal move looked attractive but failed because of a tactical detail?
4. What should the losing team remember before its next life?

The goal is explanation, not prediction accuracy. A useful wrong idea is still a useful chess lesson when the board shows why it failed.
